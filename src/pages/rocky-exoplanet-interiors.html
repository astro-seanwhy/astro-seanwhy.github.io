Interior and Atmospheric Modeling of Rocky Exoplanets

Building on these compositional insights, I expanded my research to the interior structures and early atmospheres of rocky exoplanets. On this front, I developed the ExoInt code, an open-access tool that models planetary interior structure and mineralogy from devolatilized stellar compositions. In supporting several junior researchers’ work, I particularly explored how key elemental ratios, such as C/O, Mg/Si, Fe/Mg and Eu/Mg, modulate a planet’s core size, mantle oxidation state, volatile inventory, and radiogenic heating budget. These studies reveal that planetary bulk composition plays a key role in determining planetary interiors, which in turn exert first-order control on surface and atmospheric evolution.

This part of my work further bridges theory and observation. By linking planetary bulk compositions to interior structures and volatile inventories, my studies provide a physical basis for interpreting data from current and upcoming facilities such as JWST, Roman, PLATO and ELT. With further development, these models will enable self-consistent predictions of bulk, interior and atmospheric properties and inform retrieval strategies for rocky exoplanets, an essential step toward connecting formation conditions to observable signatures and assessing planetary habitability.
