Haiyang S. Wang

Drawing on training in astrophysics and geoscience, I study how planetary systems form and evolve, and what shapes the diversity and potential habitability of rocky worlds.

My research lies at the intersection of planetary and exoplanetary science, and asks how the constituent bodies of the Solar System and other planetary systems are linked through physical and chemical processes. I have developed models, such as the volatility trend and the open-access code ExoInt, to characterize compositional relationships between stars and planets and to constrain the interior composition and structure of rocky exoplanets.

My current mission is to understand what and how the chemical and physical processes that govern planet formation and evolution shape the diversity, composition, and potential habitability of rocky worlds. My ultimate purpose is to contribute, through novel science, to advancing Earthlings’ future on the Pale Blue Dot and possibly elsewhere in the Universe.

Research themes

  • Volatile Depletion and the Star–Planet Chemical Connection

    How volatile elements are depleted in planetary materials relative to their host stars, from the empirical volatility trend to hybrid accretion.

  • Interior and Atmospheric Modeling of Rocky Exoplanets

    How bulk composition shapes the interiors, volatile inventories and early atmospheres of rocky exoplanets.
